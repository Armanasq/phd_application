## 1. Problem and perspective

A short personal guide organizes advice on choosing programs, recommendation letters, statements of purpose, contacting professors, mentorship and choosing among offers. The author explicitly warns that advice is personal and advisor preferences vary.

## 2. Useful admissions insights

- Advisor/lab fit and current funding capacity can matter more than generic university prestige.
- Apply only to programs the applicant would genuinely attend; application fees, time and SoP quality constrain portfolio size.
- Ask recommenders early whether they can write a strong research letter, not merely whether they can submit a letter.
- Tailor supervisor outreach; email is not a universal requirement.
- Do not infer too much from a supervisor's response or silence; meetings and replies are noisy signals, not admissions decisions.
- Visit days provide information about current students, faculty and the lived research environment.
