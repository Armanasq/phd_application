## Reliability, semantics and security
1. **TLS disabled:** `scraper.py` invokes `requests.get(..., verify=False)` while globally suppressing `InsecureRequestWarning`. This forfeits origin authenticity for scraped employment evidence and should never be adopted.
2. **Placeholder facts:** it hard-codes `deadline="Check PDF"` and a generic contact address for each matched link. `posted_on` is filled with the scrape date rather than the employer posting date. These are not verified application facts.
3. **False-positive extraction:** broad substring matching includes careers, program pages, announcements and internships unrelated to paid doctoral employment. The parser collects anchor text only; no official job detail retrieval, research fit, nationality constraints, stipend or application-open status check.
4. **Data-loss/freshness logic:** `INSERT OR IGNORE` on URL refuses future amendments to salary or deadline for an existing listing; there is no upsert, archive state, content hash, provenance chain or link-validity check.
5. **Error opacity:** the scraper swallows database exceptions and network failures, returning zero or skipping sources without a durable failure ledger. A silent empty scrape can be mistaken for no opportunities.
6. **Uploads and data governance:** [`app.py`](https://github.com/Gurudev517/AI-Driven-Academic-Opportunity-Portal-Using-Web-Automation-and-NLP/blob/08b002c7db7816da198891d034c24894af1007a6/app.py) constructs upload paths from client-supplied `file.filename` and saves files before resume matching; there is no proved safe filename normalization in inspected path. Tracked resume files and database artifacts create potential personal-data exposure. Do not process any uploaded resume.
7. **Maintenance:** no reproducible test suite, fixture-based scraper benchmark or license grant was identified in inspected files; provenance and dependency safety remain unverified.

## Relationship to other reviewed systems
Conceptually closer to [academic-job-digest](https://github.com/BaseLayerAI/academic-job-digest) for feed aggregation, but weaker in schema fidelity and provenance, and the career directories are mostly non-PhD-specific. [Aghababaei/PhD-Seeker](https://github.com/Aghababaei/PhD-Seeker) also demonstrated TLS/security risks; both should be treated as *anti-patterns* for direct import. The first-party vacancy evidence and structured eligibility pipeline in our project must not inherit invented deadline/contact fields.
