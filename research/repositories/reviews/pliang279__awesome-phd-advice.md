## 1. Purpose, intended audience and limits

A curated set of advice for prospective and current doctoral researchers, authored by Paul Liang with contributor acknowledgments. It spans CS/ML/NLP graduate admissions preparation, candidate statements, visit days and advisor choice; then transitions to research habits, reading, writing, peer review, teaching, mentoring, conference participation, fellowship applications, networking and job search. The collection does not list funded doctoral vacancies, prove supervisor willingness to recruit or validate admissions conditions for 2026/27. It is an organized **secondary discovery index of original advice sources**, not an admissions authority.

Evidence: [full README](https://github.com/pliang279/awesome-phd-advice/blob/1398145a30a4172eb18bbb1d7c91e4ed68e319b9/README.md).

## 3. Method and task-to-source mapping

For an applicant: map the research field and motivation -> read admissions committee and potential supervisor viewpoints -> construct a credible narrative and identify technical achievements -> choose potential lab culture/funding fit -> request recommendation letters -> independently verify application requirements -> plan interviews and submission readiness. For an enrolled researcher: build sustainable reading and writing systems -> establish novelty/contribution claims -> receive and respond to reviews -> teach/mentor, organize events and pursue grants.

The README points to both primary written guides and other curated collections, including `jedyang97/awesome-cs-phd-application-advice` and `hassony2/useful-computer-vision-phd-resources`. Those nodes should be used for *citation chaining*. Repeated references across collections are not independent corroborating admissions outcomes. A citation graph should attribute each assertion to the original authored page, retain original author, publication year and opinion/evidence level, and deduplicate normalized targets across collections.

## 4. Evidence quality, freshness and limitations

- **Historical sources:** linked titles/URLs explicitly reference 2013, 2015, 2016, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021 and 2022. These may convey enduring experience but cannot establish today’s fees, funded places, visa rules or official committee assessment.
- **Update cadence and source drift:** the inspected repository's latest commit dates to July 2024. A static README does not check whether external domains, faculty advice, fellowship rules, application prompts, or visit-day policies remain current.
- **Heterogeneous authority:** candidate autobiographies, institutional graduate admissions PDFs, blog opinions, mentor talks and conference tutorials have different reliability standards. The list does not encode a first-party authority flag, last source recheck or claim-level evidence status.
- **Regional transferability:** the content is CS/ML-centric and includes substantial US doctoral application advice. It must not silently transfer financial eligibility or admission expectations to salaried European doctoral employee positions, other countries or non-CS programmes.
- **Illustrative SoPs are not applicant facts:** the SoP resource section references public example statements and author advice. Their underlying personal achievements, publication records, research results and faculty fit belong to their authors. Copying or paraphrasing those facts as applicant experience would be fabrication.
- **Fellowships are conditionally applicable:** NSF and other fellowship guidance may have citizenship, enrollment, degree-stage or cycle restrictions; a fellowship-writing tip does not mean an international applicant is eligible or the award remains offered.
- **No outcomes benchmark:** the list does not provide an admissions sample, counterfactual, independent quality rating or calibrated odds of acceptance. Advice may be helpful but cannot be represented as a quantitative success predictor.
- **No automated source QA:** there is no source-linked content snapshot, link-health CI, publication-date verifier, program-cycle metadata, or provenance/claim ledger in the three-file tree.

These assessments are derived from the pinned Markdown and GitHub tree/history; original source documents were not read or independently verified.

## 5. Comparison with complementary research repositories

`poloclub/awesome-grad-school` offers a broader research-career checklist, organized source submissions and configured link checks; `pliang279/awesome-phd-advice` is especially coherent in distinguishing pre-application/doctoral-stage content and linking named authors’ advice. `Swapnil-Gandhi/SoP-Template` supplies one sample narrative artifact, whereas this collection supplies a menu of expert perspectives about writing SoPs. `KaiserWhoLearns/CS-PhD-Application-fee-waivers` is a policy-lead list and needs a different, stricter verification workflow. A system should not confuse citations to advice with evidence that a specific PhD offer exists.

## 7. Concrete transfer into a PhD application research workflow

A mature assistant can transform the headings into an auditable checklist: evidence-backed academic profile; research problems and contributions; labs/supervisors with official affiliation proof; LoR request ownership/deadlines; SoP fit claims checked against authentic lab publications; eligibility and funding terms verified for the correct cycle; interview readiness; and receipt-based submission tracking. Each advice recommendation should carry `origin_url`, `author`, `advice_type`, `publication_year`, `target_role`, `region`, `source_checked_at`, `factual_dependency`, and `verification_needed`.

The collection's current-PhD sections may also inform research-paper methodology, early academic development and fellowship preparation; they should not be prematurely applied as applicant selection criteria. Causal admissions claims require independent data rather than counting advisor-blog endorsements.
