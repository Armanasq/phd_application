## Nature of the repository

This is a curated reading list based on the author's 2019 application experience. It is not software, an applicant database, or a current admissions directory. Its only tracked artifact is a README with grouped links.

## Knowledge taxonomy

1. General PhD application preparation: research-oriented application strategy and how to prepare for graduate school.
2. Admissions decision-making: reflection on computer-science graduate admissions.
3. Statement of purpose: advice on narrative structure, reader expectations, and common failure modes.
4. Successful essay examples: historical SOP/personal-statement PDFs from multiple institutions and applicants.
5. Research environment selection: CSRankings as a research-output discovery aid.
6. Advisor evaluation: questions to ask a prospective PhD advisor during a visit.

## Methodological interpretation

The useful synthesis is that a strong PhD application is not merely a polished personal statement. It must connect:
- demonstrated research capability;
- a specific and credible future research direction;
- faculty/lab fit supported by actual work;
- institution-specific reasons;
- evidence of independence, ownership and research judgment.

Advisor questions add an important post-admission dimension: supervision style, group culture, student progress, publication expectations, funding continuity and research autonomy should be investigated rather than inferred from prestige.

The sample essays are comparative artifacts. Their value is in analyzing argument structure, evidence selection, specificity, research continuity and adaptation to institutional prompts. They are not reusable templates and do not establish that an applicant was admitted or funded.

## Quality and limitations

- The repository has no update process, provenance ledger, source version pinning, structured metadata, or current-cycle validation.
- Some links are historical, personal, hosted PDFs, or non-HTTPS HTTP links. Link availability and current applicability need independent checks.
- Ranking resources must not be treated as a proxy for advisor fit, lab quality, funding, or admission probability.
- Successful essays introduce survivorship and selection bias.
- The reading list is CS-centric and does not directly establish international funding/visa eligibility.
- No external linked source was treated as verified solely because it appears in the README.
