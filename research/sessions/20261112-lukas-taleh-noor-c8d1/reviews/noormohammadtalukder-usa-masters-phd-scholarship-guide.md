## 1. What the source actually contributes

The README is a first-person outline claiming a successfully funded US doctoral application. It proposes (1) university shortlisting, (2) advisor matching and individualized emails, (3) presenting unpublished research projects, and (4) assessing English-language testing and potential assistantship funding. The author self-reports receiving **2–3 replies among more than 50 emails** and no GRE submission. These are individual recollections with no cohort denominator, sampling protocol or external verification: they do **not** establish a general response probability or institution-level admission rule.

The guidance to describe an applicant's real research question, method, findings and connection to a prospective advisor is useful as a **writing heuristic**. The claim that researcher-fit matters is plausible but does not substitute for an official department's selection rubric.

## 3. Cross-repository implications

Separate `anecdotal_strategy` from `institutional_policy` and `individual_outcome_claim`. A professor outreach research record should link a concrete advisor profile, paper, actual research-method overlap, institution page, contact date, sent email receipt and reply; do not store broad "good time to send" rules as validated facts. Unpublished work should be represented faithfully (research question -> method -> experiments -> measured results -> open limitations), without inventing publications or admissions success.
