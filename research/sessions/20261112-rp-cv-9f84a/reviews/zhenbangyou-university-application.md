## 2. Transferable workflow: source-led applications rather than rankings

The guide's most useful explicit process is:
1. Clarify whether the goal is research PhD, thesis MSc or employment-oriented MSc; do not use an employment-oriented SOP for an explicitly research-directed program.
2. Build an initial university/program list and find official **program** descriptions, not infer program properties from institutional rankings.
3. Inspect curriculum, laboratories and supervisors; distinguish research productivity from admission-specific fit.
4. Track options in a spreadsheet and filter by program type, academic direction and personal constraints.
5. Group realistic applications, schedule forms and document creation early and buffer deadlines for referee action.
6. Draft a concise CV first; then build the SOP around one coherent set of experiences and the target program's purpose.
7. Use advisors, referees, lab members and peers as context, with appropriate privacy and independent verification.

The fourth chapter rightly warns that CSRankings counts scholarly outputs and may systematically favor larger faculties or publication-rich subfields. For doctoral targeting, its earlier-article appendix specifically recommends **reading supervisors' papers and lab activity**, not treating rankings as decisive. The resulting approach is useful when combined with current funded-vacancy/eligibility evidence.

## 3. Historical and potentially misleading statements

The guide reports US/Swiss 2022 cycle deadlines, TOEFL/GRE score heuristics, “GPA thresholds,” beliefs about universities preferring particular domestic schools, institutional offer counts, named-program selection formulas and perceived admissions predictability. These are **first-person observations and hearsay**, often admitted by the writer as guesses, not primary university records or current rules.

Particular claims should remain **UNVERIFIED_HISTORICAL**:
- The chapter-seven sentence that ETH/EPFL application deadlines were “12.15” describes an old particular master's cycle, not official November–December 2026 deadlines. Current named programs each require their own official source, time zone and intake cycle.
- Exact English-score heuristics (`100`, `102`, etc.) or “GRE not needed” are not universal PhD thresholds; also they cannot establish scholarship/visa eligibility.
- Suggesting that recommendation letters outweigh publication outcomes or that scholarship/awards matter little is anecdotal and department-dependent, not a reproducible global weighting model.
- Specific Stanford, CMU, UIUC, UCSD and ETH/EPFL admission counts/yields are not supported by independently verified annual institutional data in the repository.
- The tenth chapter describes a personal admissions process and no independently published final offer-to-enrollment ledger; do not derive calibrated probabilities or grade applicant confidence from it.

No claim above is “verified” simply because the Markdown exists. Cite the historical context and then obtain primary official admissions rules when acting.

## 4. Research-maturity and privacy considerations

Useful research-specific signals in the master's-oriented corpus:
- doctoral advisor/lab alignment should be investigated separately from taught-course access;
- recommenders need early notice, correct evidence and submission tracking; “letter requested” and “letter submitted” are distinct states;
- SOP should connect actual research contributions, prospective research questions and *why this lab*, not repeat CV metrics;
- a candidate with papers or strong grades is not automatically a fit for every lab.

The guide also discusses its author's social network, institution-specific admissions recollections and personal process. It is not an official dataset; avoid copying personal/student records and avoid inferring eligibility, nationality-based outcomes or current restrictions from anecdotes. Advice on drafting recommendation letters is not permission to impersonate referees; letters and actual submissions remain institution/recommender-controlled.
