## Corpus structure and patterns
The archive spans multiple universities, research areas, and degree levels. It includes both master's and PhD-oriented statements; therefore, it must not be treated as a homogeneous PhD-only corpus.

Across the statements, the recurring argument structure is:
1. Research motivation and intended direction.
2. Academic preparation and technical foundation.
3. Research or engineering projects, including methods and outputs.
4. Evidence of readiness for independent research.
5. Program-specific research alignment.
6. Longer-term research goals.

Several versions reuse a substantial common narrative while changing the program-fit section and selected experience emphasis. This demonstrates the practical value of maintaining a stable evidence-backed core, but also exposes the risk of superficial institution-specific tailoring.

## Strong reusable practices
- Describe the research problem, the applicant's own contribution, methods, and observed result rather than listing project titles.
- Connect multiple projects into a coherent research trajectory.
- Use concrete technical evidence, publications, prototypes, and measurable outcomes where available.
- Explain why the target research environment is necessary for the next step.
- Adapt the narrative to the degree level. A research master's statement and a doctoral research proposal should not use the same objective or level of research specificity.
- Keep program fit grounded in a plausible research question and the actual work of the target group.

## Weaknesses and risks observed
- Some openings rely on broad personal motivation rather than a precise research problem.
- Some statements enumerate many interests and projects without a sufficiently narrow research direction.
- Faculty references can become name-dropping when the connection to a concrete question or method is not developed.
- Repeated paragraphs create a risk that the final statement is only cosmetically tailored.
- A list of technical skills or project outcomes does not, by itself, establish research independence.
- The corpus contains no verifiable acceptance or funding outcomes. The documents cannot be labelled successful examples on the evidence available.

## Privacy and reuse constraints
The PDFs contain personal and identifying information, including academic history and research details. The recommendation letters contain third-party reference information. This report intentionally omits names, contact details, identifiers, and document-specific personal facts.

The repository has no declared license. Do not copy or redistribute its statements, recommendation letters, or personal content. Use only abstracted structural lessons.

## Operational value for the admissions workflow
Treat this repository as a private qualitative corpus, not a source of admission probabilities. A useful internal extraction schema would record:
- degree level and discipline;
- statement section structure;
- research claims and evidence type;
- contribution/method/result chain;
- specificity of program and supervisor fit;
- evidence gaps and unsupported claims;
- tailoring depth;
- date and outcome status, only if independently verified.

The strongest comparison is between the applicant's own statement versions: identify which claims remain stable, which are adapted, and whether each adaptation changes the research argument rather than only the university names.
